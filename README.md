# Magical Chocolate Co.

This project uses business data from a generator for the fictional company Magical Chocolate Co.:
https://excelx.com/practice-data/generators/business-data/

The generator has various options for choosing clean vs messy data and some data has the option of being random or having built in trends. With the exception of the data cleaning exercise, I downloaded clean data with 2000 rows and dated between 1/1/2022 and 7/17/2026. 

### Data Cleaning
As a cleaning exercise, I downloaded the Detailed Expense Ledger with 2000 rows and 15% errors. First I cleaned the data manually in Excel and then I replicated the cleaning in SQL. When dealing with duplicate rows and missing data, I prioritized retaining rows that had an expenditure amount and at least one other piece of information, so that if you were to analyze the data, you would still have the amount of the expenditures for that variable, even if it was missing other data. My cleaning methodology is described in the Excel file and the SQL query file is attached separately. The two results are not exact matches but they're reasonably close. The Excel-cleaned data has 1610 rows while the SQL-cleaned data has 1614 rows. It was more difficult in SQL than Excel to tackle the challenge of choosing which duplicate to preserve; however, with a larger dataset, the SQL queries would be the most efficient. 

### Finance & Accounting Dashboard
This dashboard has three pages based on the Detailed Expense Ledger and Accounts Receivable Aging Report. The summary page has calculated historical averages and projected measures for revenue, expenses, and profit. The column chart visualizes those three figures for the last five years. We can see that 2023 had the highest revenue and profit year. There is also a pie chart that visualizes how much revenue is sitting in unpaid customer invoices, which can be filtered by year. Lastly, there are navigation buttons to take you to the other two pages of the dashboard. 

The Departmental Expenditures page is for details on company spending by each department. There are similar callouts to the summary page for a historical average, projected spending, in addition to how much they will overspend or save compared to their historical average. All but the Quality Control and Sales departments are projected to spend more than their historical average. There is also a column chart showing historical spending and a pie chart for the breakdown of spending into categories, which can be filtered by year. 

The Overdue Invoices page is a landing spot for investigating more into the company’s unpaid customer invoices. It details how many overdue invoices there are, the total number of customers that haven’t paid their balances, and the total value of the overdue invoices. The bar chart shows how much value is in overdue invoices based on the invoice billing date. Invoices from 2024 have the highest value of overdue balances. The table on the right allows for investigating invoices by customers. You can sort this table by which customers have the most invoices overdue, the highest overdue balance, or the oldest overdue invoice. 

**<ins>Methodology:</ins>** I created a column in both tables to isolate the year of the invoice and expenditures so that I could link the tables based on time and would be able to filter the data based on year. In the Accounts Receivable table, I created a revenue column that assigned $0 to unpaid invoices. I also created a column based on if the invoice was unpaid and past its due date with either Yes, No, or Null. I also calculated a column for the number of days the invoice was past due. I then created a new table based on the overdue invoices column so that I could isolate the data for all overdue invoices, which informs the third page of the dashboard. I also created several measures which can all be found in the callout cards on each of the dashboard pages.  

### Retail Sales & Product Review Dashboard
This dashboard was built to provide insights into the sales and customer feedback of individual products for this company. On the summary page, there are company totals for number of units sold, average price per unit, and overall revenue, as well as the average review score for all products. The bar graph on the right demonstrates that product P103 brings in the most revenue. We can also see that product P101 has one of the lowest sales numbers but its unit price must make up for it because it lands in the top five revenue earners. The line graph on the bottom left of the page is a growth rate chart for sales, revenue, and number of customers. We can see from the trend lines that there is some modest growth over the last five years, with a spike occurring just recently. Lastly, there are navigation buttons to take you to the other two pages of this dashboard. 

The Sales page has a product filter so that we can see their effect on sales for the company. Similar to the summary page, there are quick statistics on unit sales and price and total revenue. The line graph on the right displays seasonal spikes. Total sales spike in November, December, and February; however, using the filter, we can also see product specific trends. For example, product P101 seems to also have modest sales spikes in May and June. The bottom right graph demonstrates that the average unit price per product is always lowest in Latin America, but that total revenue varies by region for each product. The last graph on the left shows which sales representative is bringing in the most revenue for the company and from which type of customer. Sales Rep 3 is the best with VIP clients while Sales Rep 1 is better at returning customers, but Sales Rep 5 brings in the most revenue. When looking at individual products, Sales Rep 2 brings in the most revenue for product P103, especially from returning clients. For product C303, some of the lower performing sales representatives do surprisingly well with returning customers.

The Reviews page also has a product filter and an additional filter for review scores. This is especially useful with the pie chart on the right. For example, when looking at reviews for product C302 with a score between 1 and 2, the dominant feedback is “melted during shipping” and “not worth the price.” The table next to the filters lists each product and its average rating. Product C302 has the highest score with the runner up being much closer to the total average of 2.99. The graph on the bottom left was created to see if there was a relationship between the number of reviews each customer leaves and their average score. Overall, the trend line seems to stay mostly flat and close to the average. Some of the products do trend a little bit in either direction, suggesting that for that product, the more reviews a customer leaves, the higher they rate the product or vice versa for a downward trend. The last chart on the right displays average review ratings over time. For all products together, reviews are trending down; however, products C303, P102, and P201, scores trend positively. 

**<ins>Methodology:</ins>** This data did not require much transformation. I updated a few of the columns to the currency type. For the growth chart on the summary page, I created previous day measures for each of the three categories that would sum or count the previous day totals. Then I calculated the percent change, while accounting for blank information or zeroes.
